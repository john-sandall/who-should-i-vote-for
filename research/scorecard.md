# Islington Labour: 2022 Manifesto vs 2022–2026 Delivery

A neutral attempt to score Labour's 2022 council manifesto ("Fairer, Safer, Greener Together: Building a more equal Islington") against what's on the public record up to May 2026.

**Methodology**
- Pledges drawn from publicly-stated 2022 manifesto positions and supporting Labour communications.
- Delivery evidence taken from: Islington Council annual reports / press releases, Housing Ombudsman rulings, council budget papers, *Islington Tribune* and *Islington Gazette* coverage, and Labour's own 2026 manifesto (which restates progress).
- Scores: ✅ Largely delivered · 🟡 Partially delivered / mixed · 🔴 Not delivered or regressed · ⬜ Insufficient public evidence to score.
- Where Labour cites a number and there's no countervailing evidence, the number is taken as stated.

## Housing

| 2022 pledge | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 750 new council homes (within 1,550 affordable/social total) | 🟡 | Council says "largest building programme in a generation" delivered; 392 social-rent homes delivered since 2022 via planning. Greens contest the ratio: claim 18 private homes built per 1 council home in current pipeline. |
| Buy back ex-Right-to-Buy homes | ✅ | 660 homes bought back since 2022 (target 907 by March 2026). Conservatives put own-funds spend at £89m. |
| Spend £10m+/year on insulation/energy efficiency | 🟡 | Labour 2026 says "delivered" but no audited public total located; new £388m 2026–30 commitment effectively re-pledges this. |
| Improved housing repairs | 🔴 | **Housing Ombudsman special report (Oct 2023)** found severe maladministration rate of 24.7% — ~4× national average. 89 findings across 30 determinations; resident complaint upheld in *every* case. Repairs delays, ASB handling, complaint handling all cited. Council accepted all recommendations. **2024–2025 Ombudsman rulings** continued to find delays (e.g. case 202336503: roof leak unresolved Nov 2022 – Jul 2023; £1,892 redress). The 2026 manifesto explicitly re-pledges fixes. |
| Crack down on housing fraud / illegal sub-letting | ✅ | Continued enforcement; restated and expanded in 2026. |
| Help residents downsize | ✅ | Downsizing team operating; financial incentives; expansion pledged again 2026. |
| Pursue rogue private landlords | 🟡 | Selective licensing scheme operating; expansion pledged. Lib Dems & Conservatives both critical of the model from different angles. |

## Council finances and governance

| 2022 pledge | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sound, balanced budget | ✅ | Balanced budgets set 2024/25 and 2025/26 *despite* £10.8m savings required for 24/25 alone. Council reports core spending power down 45% since 2010 (a national trend, not Islington-specific). |
| Council tax max-allowed rises | 🟡 | Continued at 2.99% + 2% adult social care precept (24/25). Conservatives argue this leaves Islington's bills among the highest in London (~2× Wandsworth/Westminster). Lib Dems pledged not to raise above inflation. |
| In-source key services | ✅ | Repairs service brought back in-house. "In-source first" approach formally adopted. |
| Listening to residents | 🔴 | Both opposition groupings (Greens, ICI) campaign explicitly on a perceived listening / democratic deficit. The Housing Ombudsman special report described "limited [resident] notice" and weak complaint promotion. |

## Party-specific defections / political health

- **Four Labour councillors** (Cinko-Oner, Graham, Nathan, Shaikh) have defected to form **ICI**. By their own statements this was over Gaza, the two-child cap, the Corbyn affair and an internal-democracy critique. This is not a manifesto-delivery question per se but is highly relevant to Labour's record on internal cohesion.
- Cllr Sara Hyde (Labour, Caledonian ward — *not* Bunhill) was elevated to the **House of Lords** as Baroness Hyde of Bemerton on 12 January 2026; she stepped down at the regular May 2026 elections rather than triggering a separate by-election.
- Cllr Una O'Halloran took over as Council Leader in November 2024.

## Overall scorecard summary (this researcher's reading)

- **Strongest delivery:** RTB buyback, free school meals, Year 7 device, Council Tax Support scheme, in-sourcing.
- **Weakest delivery:** Housing repairs (Housing Ombudsman special report is severe), perception of listening / democratic engagement, fly-tipping/cleaning headline numbers.
- **Mixed:** Council homebuilding (some progress, ratio criticised), LTNs (delivered but contested), youth services (continued but pressured), climate (slow on the timetable).
- **Insufficient public evidence:** Several economy/jobs targets where audited delivery figures aren't published.

The fairest summary for a Bunhill voter: Labour can credibly point to several headline deliverables (especially in housing supply, RTB buyback, schools, in-sourcing). They have a serious, externally-validated weakness on housing repairs and complaint handling. The 2026 manifesto's heavy emphasis on repairs, lifts, and a "10-day in-touch guarantee" is itself an admission of where this term fell short.
